Does a stock near its 200-week average do better?

What is this?

Some people say: when a stock falls close to its 200-week average, it is a good time to buy. The 200-week average is just the average price over the last 200 weeks, about four years. It is a line on a price chart.

We checked if that idea really works. We looked at strong, well-run US companies. We compared the ones sitting near that line with similar ones that were not, in the same week. Then we saw who made more money over the next one, three and five years.

What would have happened to $10,000?

The short answer

The data cannot tell.

The data cannot say whether buying quality companies near their 200-week average helps. Too many missing companies. The range of what the data cannot rule out runs from -6.3 to 2.6 points a year, so the data does not settle the question. The weekly list is shown for interest, and a forward scoreboard will test it as new years arrive.

  • In plain words: we could not prove the idea works, and we could not prove it fails.
  • Why: the free price data we can use leaves out many companies that later dropped out of the index. About 39% of the weeks are missing in the first test and 25% in the second. That is too many for a firm answer.
  • What it hints at: Cannot tell does not mean no harm. In 6 of 6 test and holding-time pairs the best guess was below zero, and the ranges still include zero or a small gain. Random picks did better than the dip picks in most draws for 6 of 6 pairs.

So please do not read this page as advice to buy or avoid anything. It is a test of one idea, nothing more.

The numbers

"Gap" is how many percentage points a year the near-the-line stocks earned more than the others, after trading costs. Below zero means they did worse. "Range" is what the data cannot rule out. If the range includes zero, there may be no real gap. "Price only" looks at all large companies. "Quality companies" looks only at strong, well-run ones. "Weeks" is how many weeks the idea gave a buy signal.
